# Campaign portfolio management

Six services acting on the same customer base will collide unless the portfolio is managed as one
system. Work the four checks below before any campaign goes to market.

## 1. Collision map

Build the grid before approving the calendar:

| Campaign | Service | Brands/SKUs | Territory | Customer segment | Window | Collides with |
| -------- | ------- | ----------- | --------- | ---------------- | ------ | ------------- |

Three collisions matter most:

- **Same customer, same window, two incentives** — inflated response, attribution unusable.
- **Promotion on a SKU while pricing is testing elasticity on it** — both reads are destroyed.
- **Demand generation on a SKU with stock cover under the campaign window** — spend buys stockouts.

## 2. Cannibalization and pull-forward

For every promotion or price move, state the expected split between incremental volume,
switched volume from an adjacent pack of our own portfolio, and volume pulled forward from the
next period. Only the first is a gain. Power couples are protected: do not run deep discounts on
the highest-EBITDA combinations to hit a volume target.

## 3. Sequencing

Order plays so each one's read stays clean:

1. **Structural first** — assortment and PPA changes set the ladder everything else runs on.
   These sit **outside this system's services**: you sequence around them and flag when they are
   pending, you do not plan or brief them.
2. **Price second** — establish the elasticity read before promotions distort it.
3. **Demand third** — advertisements against the corrected ladder.
4. **Value capture last** — recommendations and promotions on the traffic the demand plays bought.

Leave a clean window between a test and the next play on the same scope, long enough for the
measurement lag.

## 4. One measurement plan

The portfolio has a single holdout design, not one per campaign. Overlapping holdouts produce
contaminated controls. Decide up front which effects are read separately and which are read as a
combined lift, and record that in every brief so sub-agents do not each invent a control group.

## Portfolio review cadence

Each checkpoint: KPI vs target, spend vs plan, significance status, and one decision per campaign —
continue, resize, redesign, or stop. Stopping is a valid outcome and frees budget for reallocation.
